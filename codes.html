About these codes

ICD-10 is the World Health Organization's International Classification of Diseases, 10th revision. Many countries use their own detailed versions. The codes above follow the U.S. clinical modification, ICD-10-CM, which adds a final digit for whether a headache is intractable. Always check against the release in force for your country and year; the U.S. code set updates every 1 October.

What "intractable" means

In ICD-10-CM, intractable means the headache is hard to treat: pharmacoresistant, treatment-resistant, refractory or poorly controlled. It is not a judgement about how severe the pain is.

G44 is a header

G44 itself, and its subheadings such as G44.0 or G44.2, are categories. Claims use the most specific code that matches the documented diagnosis, such as G44.209.

What G44 excludes

Migraine has its own category (G43). Headache with no specific diagnosis is R51.9. Trigeminal neuralgia (G50.0), atypical facial pain (G50.1) and headache after a lumbar puncture (G97.1) are coded elsewhere.

ICD-11

In the newer ICD-11, primary headache disorders sit in the 8A80 to 8A8Z range; for example tension-type headache is 8A81 and the trigeminal autonomic cephalalgias, including cluster headache, are 8A82.
