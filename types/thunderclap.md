> **If this is happening now, call your local emergency number** (911 in the US and Canada, 999 in the UK, 112 in the EU and India). Do not drive yourself. Do not wait to see whether it passes.

A thunderclap headache is severe head pain that reaches its peak **within about a minute** and lasts at least five minutes. People often call it the worst headache of their life. Even if it eases, it needs same-hour medical assessment, because it can be the first sign of bleeding or a blood-vessel problem in or around the brain.

## What doctors look for

- **Subarachnoid haemorrhage**, often from a ruptured aneurysm.
- **Reversible cerebral vasoconstriction syndrome (RCVS)**: brief narrowing of brain arteries, often with repeated thunderclap headaches over days or weeks. It can follow exertion, sex, straining, childbirth, or some medicines and drugs, such as decongestants or cannabis.
- **Cervical artery dissection**: a tear in a neck artery.
- **Cerebral venous thrombosis**: a clot in a brain vein.
- **Pituitary apoplexy**, stroke, very high blood pressure and spontaneous low spinal-fluid pressure.

Tests usually start with a CT scan, sometimes followed by a lumbar puncture and scans of the blood vessels.

## Primary thunderclap headache

Only when every serious cause has been excluded do doctors use the diagnosis **primary thunderclap headache** (G44.53). It is a diagnosis of exclusion and must never be assumed at home.

## What to tell the emergency team

- Exactly when it started and what you were doing.
- How fast it peaked: "seconds" or "within a minute".
- Any neck stiffness, vomiting, fainting, vision change, weakness or confusion.
- Recent new medicines, decongestants, recreational drugs, or recent childbirth.
