If you usually have caffeine every day, missing it can cause a headache. The international classification describes it in people who have taken **more than 200 mg a day for more than two weeks**: the headache starts within 24 hours of the last dose, is relieved within an hour by about 100 mg of caffeine, and fades within a week if caffeine stays off.

## Typical timeline

In research reviews, symptoms usually start 12 to 24 hours after the last dose, peak between about 20 and 51 hours, and can last from 2 to 9 days. Tiredness, low mood and poor concentration often come with the headache.

## Common situations

- **Weekend headaches** when you sleep in and have your first coffee late.
- **Fasting** for a blood test, surgery or religious reasons.
- **Travel** across time zones, or simply a busy morning without a cup.

## How much caffeine is in what

| Drink | Typical caffeine |
| --- | --- |
| Brewed coffee, 240 ml (8 oz) | about 95 mg |
| Espresso, single shot | about 63 mg |
| Black tea, 240 ml | about 47 mg |
| Cola, 355 ml (12 oz) | about 34 mg |
| Energy drink, 250 ml | about 80 mg |

The U.S. FDA says up to 400 mg a day is not generally linked to dangerous effects in healthy adults. For people with frequent headaches, regular high intake also adds to [medication-overuse risk](types/medication-overuse.html). ## Cutting back without a headache

- Reduce by about a quarter every few days rather than stopping at once.
- Swap one cup at a time for half-caffeinated or decaf.
- Keep your first caffeine at the same time every day, weekends included.
- Watch hidden sources: some headache tablets contain caffeine.
