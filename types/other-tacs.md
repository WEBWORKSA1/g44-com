These conditions are cousins of [cluster headache](types/cluster.html). All of them cause pain on one side of the head, with symptoms such as a red or watery eye, a blocked or runny nostril, or a drooping eyelid on the same side. They differ mainly in how long each attack lasts.

| Condition | Attack length | How often | Key clue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paroxysmal hemicrania | 2 to 30 minutes | Usually 5 or more a day | Stops completely with indomethacin |
| SUNCT and SUNA | 1 second to 10 minutes | Many a day, sometimes hundreds | Can be set off by touching the face or chewing |
| Hemicrania continua | Constant, with flare-ups | Every day for 3 months or more | Stops completely with indomethacin |

## Paroxysmal hemicrania

Attacks look like short, frequent cluster attacks: severe one-sided pain around the eye or temple lasting minutes, many times a day. Unlike cluster headache, it affects women at least as often as men. The defining feature is an **absolute response to indomethacin**, an anti-inflammatory medicine: the attacks stop. Doctors use a supervised trial of indomethacin to confirm the diagnosis. It can upset the stomach, so it is taken with protective medicine under medical care.

## SUNCT and SUNA

The names stand for short-lasting unilateral neuralgiform headache attacks with conjunctival injection and tearing (SUNCT) or with cranial autonomic symptoms (SUNA). Attacks are very brief stabs or series of stabs around one eye. Because touching the face or chewing can set them off, they are often confused with trigeminal neuralgia. Indomethacin does not help. Neurologists use other medicines, and a brain scan is important to rule out a cause near the pituitary gland or the nerve.

## Hemicrania continua

This is a **constant one-sided headache** that never fully goes away, with flare-ups of more severe pain and eye or nose symptoms on that side. Many people also feel restless during flare-ups. Like paroxysmal hemicrania, it **responds completely to indomethacin**. It is easy to mislabel as chronic migraine, so the indomethacin trial matters.

## What to do

These are specialist diagnoses. If your pain is always on one side with eye or nose symptoms, see a neurologist and bring a diary of attack lengths and timings. Our [type finder](tools/type-finder.html) and [diary](tools/headache-diary.html) help you describe the pattern precisely, which is most of the diagnosis.
