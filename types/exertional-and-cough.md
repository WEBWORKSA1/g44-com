These three headaches are grouped because each is set off by a specific trigger. All can be harmless "primary" headaches, but all can also be the first sign of something serious. That is why **the first episode always needs a medical assessment**.

| Type | Trigger | How long | First-time check for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary cough headache (G44.83) | Coughing, sneezing, straining, laughing | 1 second to 2 hours | A Chiari malformation or other problem at the base of the skull |
| Primary exertional headache (G44.84) | Hard physical exercise | Under 48 hours | Bleeding, an artery tear or RCVS |
| Sexual-activity headache (G44.82) | Sexual activity; may build or explode at climax | 1 minute to 24 hours (mild ache up to 72) | The same as a thunderclap headache |

## Cough headache

Pain comes on suddenly with the cough or strain and fades within minutes. According to the international classification, about 40% of cough headaches have an underlying cause, most often a Chiari malformation, where the lower brain sits low in the skull opening. A brain MRI is usually recommended.

## Exertional headache

It often throbs and is linked to sustained, strenuous effort, especially in heat or at altitude. A first exertional headache, or one that comes on suddenly, needs urgent checks for a bleed or an artery tear.

## Sexual-activity headache

It can be a dull ache that builds with excitement, or a sudden explosive pain at climax. The explosive form can look exactly like a [thunderclap headache](types/thunderclap.html), so the first one is an emergency until proven otherwise.

## Prevention once serious causes are excluded

- Warm up gradually, stay hydrated and avoid exercising hard in heat or at altitude.
- A doctor may suggest a medicine such as indomethacin taken beforehand, or a beta-blocker for frequent episodes.
- Treat a persistent cough.

Many people find these headaches fade within months to a few years.
