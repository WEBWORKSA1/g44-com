Cluster headache is rare, affecting fewer than 1 adult in 1,000, but the pain is so intense that people often call it the worst they have ever felt. It belongs to a group of headaches called trigeminal autonomic cephalalgias, named after the facial nerve and the automatic eye and nose symptoms that come with them.

## What an attack is like

- **Severe, one-sided pain** around or behind one eye or at the temple. People describe it as boring, burning or stabbing.
- Each attack lasts **15 minutes to 3 hours** if untreated.
- On the **same side**: a red or watery eye, a blocked or runny nostril, a drooping or swollen eyelid, a small pupil, or a sweaty forehead.
- **Restlessness.** Most people pace, rock or press on their head. People with migraine tend to lie still.
- **Clockwork timing.** Attacks often come at the same time each day, frequently waking people an hour or two after they fall asleep. Bouts often return in the same season.

## Episodic and chronic

In **episodic** cluster headache, bouts last from a week to a year and are separated by pain-free gaps of at least three months. In **chronic** cluster headache, attacks continue for a year or more with no break, or breaks shorter than three months. Most people have the episodic form.

## Treating an attack

These treatments are prescribed by a doctor:

- **High-flow oxygen:** 100% oxygen through a non-rebreathing mask at 12 to 15 litres a minute for 15 to 20 minutes.
- **Triptan injection or nasal spray**, such as sumatriptan injection or zolmitriptan nasal spray.

Over-the-counter pain relievers and tablets act too slowly for most attacks.

## Preventing attacks during a bout

A neurologist may start a preventive medicine as a bout begins. Verapamil is widely used and needs heart monitoring. A short course of steroids or a nerve block at the back of the head can bridge the gap while it takes effect. In the United States, galcanezumab is approved for episodic cluster headache. Nerve-stimulation devices are another option for some people.

## Living with it

- Avoid alcohol during a bout. Even small amounts can set off an attack.
- Keep sleep times regular, and keep oxygen where you sleep if it is prescribed.
- Connect with others: [OUCH UK](https://ouchuk.org/) and [Clusterbusters](https://clusterbusters.org/) run support groups.

The pain can be overwhelming. If it ever leaves you thinking about ending your life, call or text 988 in the US and Canada, call 116 123 (Samaritans) in the UK and Ireland, or 14416 (Tele-MANAS) in India, or your local emergency number.

## Getting diagnosed

Cluster headache is often mistaken for sinus, dental or migraine pain, sometimes for years. Diagnosis rests on your description, so log attack times in a [headache diary](tools/headache-diary.html). Doctors usually arrange a brain scan to rule out other causes. Ask for a neurologist referral if your attacks fit this pattern.
