Tension-type headache is the everyday headache: the one people mean when they say "just a headache". The World Health Organization notes that in some populations more than 70% of people report the episodic form. It is rarely dangerous, but when it comes often it can wear down work, sleep and mood.

## What it feels like

- A **pressing or tightening** feeling, like a band or a heavy hat. It does not throb.
- On **both sides** of the head.
- **Mild to moderate.** You can usually keep going.
- **Not made worse** by walking or climbing stairs.
- **No nausea.** At most, light *or* sound bothers you, not both.

If your headache throbs, makes you feel sick, and sends you to a dark room, read about [migraine](types/migraine.html) instead. Many people have both.

## How often: episodic or chronic

| Form | How often | Code |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Infrequent episodic | Less than 1 day a month | G44.219 |
| Frequent episodic | 1 to 14 days a month, for more than 3 months | G44.219 |
| Chronic | 15 or more days a month, for more than 3 months | G44.229 |

## What causes it

The exact cause is not fully understood. Many people have tender muscles around the head, jaw and neck. In the chronic form, the nervous system seems to become more sensitive to pain signals. Common triggers include stress, short or broken sleep, missed meals, too little water, long hours at a screen with the head pushed forward, and clenching the jaw.

## What helps right now

- A standard dose of a simple pain reliever such as paracetamol (acetaminophen), ibuprofen or aspirin, as the label directs.
- A glass of water and something to eat.
- Warmth or a cold pack on the neck and shoulders for 15 minutes.
- A short walk, gentle neck stretches, or a few minutes of [slow breathing](tools/breathing.html).

Keep count of pain-relief days. Simple pain relievers on 15 or more days a month can turn tension-type headache into [medication-overuse headache](types/medication-overuse.html).

## Preventing it

- Keep sleep, meals and water steady, and move every day.
- Set up your desk so your screen is at eye level. Our [screens and desks guide](guides/screens-and-desks.html) shows how.
- Physiotherapy, relaxation training, cognitive behavioural therapy and biofeedback all have evidence for frequent and chronic forms.
- For chronic tension-type headache, the UK's NICE guidance says a course of up to 10 acupuncture sessions over 5 to 8 weeks can be considered.
- A doctor may suggest a preventive medicine. Low-dose amitriptyline is the most studied.

## When to see a doctor

Book an appointment if headaches come more than a few days a week, if you need pain relief on more than 10 days a month, if the pattern changes, or if a new headache starts after age 50. A [headache diary](tools/headache-diary.html) makes that visit far more useful.
