Primary stabbing headache, often called ice-pick headache, is a **single stab or a series of stabs** that each last **a few seconds** and then vanish. The jabs come at irregular times, from once a day to many times, with nothing in between.

## Typical features

- Sharp, sudden, brief pain, often around the eye, temple or the side of the head.
- The spot may move from place to place.
- **No** red or watery eye, runny nose or drooping eyelid. If those are present, think of [SUNCT](types/other-tacs.html) instead.
- More common in people who also have [migraine](types/migraine.html) or [cluster headache](types/cluster.html).

## Is it serious?

Usually not. Most people need no treatment beyond reassurance. Get it checked if:

- the stabs **always hit exactly the same spot**;
- they started after a head injury or come with other new symptoms;
- they are getting more frequent or more intense.

## Treatment

Attacks are too short for a painkiller to work. If stabs are frequent and distressing, a doctor may suggest indomethacin or melatonin for a period. Logging them in a [headache diary](tools/headache-diary.html) helps show whether they cluster around other headaches or triggers.
