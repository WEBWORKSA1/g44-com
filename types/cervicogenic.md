In cervicogenic headache, the pain is felt in the head but the source is in the neck: the joints, discs or soft tissues of the upper spine. Nerves from the upper neck share a relay station in the brainstem with nerves from the head, so neck problems can be felt as headache.

## Typical features

- Pain that **starts in the neck or at the back of the head** and spreads forward, often to the forehead or around the eye.
- Usually on **one side**, and the same side each time.
- **Neck movement or certain postures** bring it on or make it worse.
- Reduced movement in the neck.

To make the diagnosis, the classification asks for evidence that the neck problem causes the headache: for example, the headache began with the neck disorder, eases when the neck improves, or stops with a numbing injection in the neck.

## Look-alikes

Neck pain is also common in [migraine](types/migraine.html) and [tension-type headache](types/tension-type.html), so neck pain alone does not prove a neck cause. Occipital neuralgia, with shooting pains in the scalp, is another neighbour.

## Treatment

- **Physiotherapy** that combines manual therapy with exercises for the deep neck and shoulder muscles has the best evidence.
- **Posture and workstation changes**, especially for long screen days. See our [screens and desks guide](guides/screens-and-desks.html).
- **Nerve blocks** can confirm the diagnosis and give relief. In selected people who respond, specialists may offer radiofrequency treatment.
- Pain relievers have a limited role. Keep an eye on [rebound risk](tools/rebound-risk.html).

## When it is urgent

Neck pain with fever, after a fall or crash, or with arm weakness, numbness or trouble walking needs urgent medical care.
