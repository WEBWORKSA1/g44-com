Hypnic headache **only happens during sleep** and wakes you up, often at a consistent time, which is why it is called the alarm-clock headache. It is uncommon and usually begins after the age of 50.

## How to recognise it

The international criteria describe:

- headache that develops only during sleep and causes waking;
- on **10 or more days a month** for more than 3 months;
- lasting from **15 minutes up to 4 hours** after waking;
- **no** red or watery eye, runny nose or restlessness.

The pain is usually mild to moderate and often on both sides. That last point helps separate it from [cluster headache](types/cluster.html), which also wakes people but is one-sided, severe and comes with eye and nose symptoms.

## Rule out other causes first

A new headache after 50 always deserves a medical check. Night-time headaches can also come from:

- sleep apnoea, often with snoring and morning headache;
- high blood pressure at night;
- [medication overuse](types/medication-overuse.html) or caffeine withdrawal;
- low blood sugar overnight in people on diabetes medicines;
- rarely, a problem inside the head.

Your doctor may check blood pressure, ask about snoring, review medicines and arrange a scan.

## Treatment

The best-known treatment is surprisingly simple: **caffeine at bedtime**, as a cup of coffee or a caffeine tablet, helps many people and usually does not stop them sleeping. Doctors may also try lithium, indomethacin or melatonin. Each needs medical supervision, especially lithium.

## Track the timing

A [headache diary](tools/headache-diary.html) that records the time you wake with pain is the most useful thing you can bring to your appointment.
