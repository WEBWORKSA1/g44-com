Migraine is a genetic neurological disease, not "just a headache". The World Health Organization ranks it among the leading causes of disability worldwide. It sits next door to the G44 family in the classification, with its own code, G43, and it is the headache most often mistaken for [tension-type](types/tension-type.html), [sinus](types/sinus.html) or [cluster](types/cluster.html) headache.

## How an attack unfolds

1. **Prodrome**, hours to a day before: yawning, food cravings, mood change, neck stiffness.
2. **Aura**, in about a third of people: zigzag lines, flashing lights or blind spots, sometimes tingling or speech trouble, building over minutes and lasting up to an hour.
3. **Headache**, 4 to 72 hours: moderate to severe, often one-sided and throbbing, worse with movement, with nausea and sensitivity to light and sound.
4. **Postdrome**: feeling drained or foggy for up to a day.

If you have headache on 15 or more days a month, with migraine features on at least 8, doctors call it **chronic migraine**.

## Treating attacks

- Take treatment **early** in the attack.
- Options include anti-inflammatory pain relievers, triptans, and newer medicines called gepants and ditans, prescribed by a doctor.
- Rest in a dark, quiet room. Anti-sickness medicine can help.
- Keep treatment days within limits to avoid [medication-overuse headache](types/medication-overuse.html).

## Preventing attacks

If attacks are frequent or disabling, ask about prevention. Options include long-standing tablets (such as certain blood-pressure medicines, anticonvulsants and antidepressants), monthly or quarterly injections or infusions that block CGRP, daily gepant tablets, and Botox injections for chronic migraine. Lifestyle steady-state helps too: Mayo Clinic's SEEDS approach stands for sleep, exercise, eating regularly, a diary and stress management.

## Get help urgently if

An aura lasts more than an hour, comes with weakness on one side, or your "migraine" is suddenly different from usual. These need same-day assessment.
