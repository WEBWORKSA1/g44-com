Headache is the most common symptom after a concussion. Post-traumatic headache is defined by timing: it starts **within 7 days** of the injury, or within 7 days of regaining consciousness or the ability to report pain. It can follow a fall, a sports collision, a car crash, an assault or whiplash, even when no scan shows damage.

## Emergency signs after a head injury

Go to an emergency department or call your local emergency number if, after a head injury, there is:

- a headache that keeps getting worse;
- repeated vomiting, drowsiness or confusion;
- weakness, numbness, slurred speech or a seizure;
- clear fluid from the nose or ears, or new deafness;
- unequal pupils or loss of consciousness.

People on blood thinners and older adults should get checked after any significant head knock.

## Acute and persistent

| Form | Lasts | Code |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acute post-traumatic headache | Gone within 3 months | G44.31x |
| Persistent (chronic) post-traumatic headache | More than 3 months | G44.32x |

Most often the headache looks like [migraine](types/migraine.html) or [tension-type headache](types/tension-type.html). Neck pain, dizziness, poor sleep, trouble concentrating and low mood often come with it.

## What helps

- **Short rest, then gradual return.** One or two days of relative rest, then slowly build back light activity. Long periods of total rest tend to slow recovery.
- **Treat the pattern.** Doctors treat migraine-like post-traumatic headache with migraine treatments, and tension-like headache with tension-type approaches.
- **Watch pain-relief days.** People with post-traumatic headache are especially prone to [medication-overuse headache](types/medication-overuse.html).
- **Physiotherapy** for the neck, and vestibular therapy for dizziness.
- **Sleep and mood care.** Both strongly affect recovery.

## Keep records

If the injury happened in a crash, at work or because of someone else's actions, keep a [headache diary](tools/headache-diary.html) from the start and keep copies of medical notes.
