People with new daily persistent headache (NDPH) can usually name the day it began: "It started on 3 March and never stopped." The pain becomes **continuous within 24 hours** of that start and stays for **more than 3 months**. Many people had few headaches before.

## What it is like

The headache itself can feel like [tension-type headache](types/tension-type.html), like [migraine](types/migraine.html), or a mix of both. What defines NDPH is not the feel but the story: a sudden, remembered start and no break since.

## Possible triggers

The cause is not known. Many people link the start to:

- a viral or flu-like infection;
- a stressful life event;
- surgery or a medical procedure.

For many, there is no obvious trigger at all.

## What doctors rule out

Because a sudden, constant headache can have other causes, doctors check for secondary causes first, such as a spinal-fluid leak (often worse when upright), raised pressure in the head, a clot in a brain vein, infection, or [medication overuse](types/medication-overuse.html). Expect a neurological examination and, often, a brain scan.

## Management

There is no single proven treatment. Specialists usually:

- treat according to the headache's features, using migraine or tension-type treatments;
- start a preventive medicine and give each one a fair trial;
- limit acute pain-relief days to avoid medication overuse;
- add physiotherapy, psychological support and sleep care;
- refer to a headache clinic if early treatments fail.

Some people improve within months; others need longer-term care. Tracking every day in a [headache diary](tools/headache-diary.html) shows whether a treatment is working.

## When to get help fast

If the headache started like a thunderclap, worsens when you stand, or comes with fever, weakness or vision changes, seek urgent care. See the [warning-sign checker](tools/red-flags.html).
