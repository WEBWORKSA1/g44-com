The rule behind the check

The International Classification of Headache Disorders (ICHD-3) describes medication-overuse headache as headache on 15 or more days a month in someone with an existing headache disorder, plus regular overuse for more than 3 months of acute headache medicine: simple pain relievers on 15 or more days a month, or triptans, ergots, opioids or combination pain relievers on 10 or more days a month, or any mix of these on 10 or more days.

The key word is days. Two tablets on one day count as one day. Taking something on most days, even at low doses, is what drives the problem.

Read more in our guide to medication-overuse headache. Never stop opioids, barbiturate-containing medicines or sleeping tablets suddenly without medical advice.
