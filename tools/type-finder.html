How the finder works

Doctors diagnose most headaches from the story: where the pain is, what it feels like, how long it lasts, how often it comes and what comes with it. The International Classification of Headache Disorders (ICHD-3) turns those features into criteria. The finder asks the same questions and scores your answers against the typical features of each headache in the G44 family and its neighbours.

Your answers stay in your browser. The result is a pattern match, not a diagnosis. Many people have more than one type of headache, and some serious causes can look like common ones. Take your result to a clinician, ideally with a headache diary.

Questions that matter most

  • Attack length separates the types more than anything else: seconds (stabbing), minutes to 3 hours (cluster family), hours to days (migraine, tension-type), or constant.
  • Eye and nose symptoms on one side point strongly to the cluster family.
  • Nausea and light sensitivity point to migraine.
  • A sudden, worst-ever start is an emergency, whatever else is true.
