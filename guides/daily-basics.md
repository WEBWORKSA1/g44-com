The headache brain likes routine. Big swings in sleep, food, fluids and caffeine are among the most commonly reported triggers for both [migraine](types/migraine.html) and [tension-type headache](types/tension-type.html). The aim is not perfection. It is steadiness.

## Sleep

- Go to bed and get up at about the same time every day, **including weekends**. A long lie-in is a classic weekend-headache trigger.
- Most adults need 7 to 9 hours.
- Keep screens out of the last half hour before bed.
- Snoring, gasping or morning headaches can point to sleep apnoea. Mention them to your doctor.

## Meals

- Eat at regular times and do not skip breakfast.
- Include some protein and fibre to keep blood sugar steady.
- Carry a snack for busy days.
- Food triggers are real for some people but are often overestimated. Track for at least two months before cutting foods out.

## Water

- Sip through the day rather than drinking a lot at once.
- Pale-yellow urine is a simple sign you are drinking enough.
- Drink more in heat, during exercise and when travelling.

## Caffeine

- Keep your intake steady from day to day, and have your first caffeine at the same time every morning.
- If you get frequent headaches, aim to stay below about 200 mg a day: roughly two cups of brewed coffee.
- Cut back gradually if you want to reduce it, to avoid [withdrawal headaches](types/caffeine-withdrawal.html). The [caffeine counter](tools/caffeine-counter.html) adds it up for you.

## Two more that matter

- **Move.** Regular, moderate exercise reduces headache frequency for many people. Start gently and warm up.
- **Manage stress.** Short daily habits, such as [slow breathing](tools/breathing.html), walks and time off screens, help more than occasional big efforts.

Mayo Clinic sums these up as **SEEDS**: sleep, exercise, eat, diary and stress. A [headache diary](tools/headache-diary.html) is how you find out which of them matter most for you.
