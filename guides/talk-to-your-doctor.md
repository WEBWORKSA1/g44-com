There is no blood test or scan that diagnoses most headaches. Doctors diagnose them from your story. A clear story, told well, is the most powerful thing you can bring.

## Before the appointment

- **Keep a diary** for at least four weeks, ideally two to three months. Our [headache diary](tools/headache-diary.html) does the counting for you.
- **List every medicine you take**, including over-the-counter pain relievers, supplements, contraceptives and anything taken "only sometimes". Note how many days a month you take each pain reliever.
- **Describe a typical attack**: where it hurts, what it feels like, how long it lasts, what comes with it, and what you do during it.
- **Note family history.** Migraine in particular runs in families.
- **Decide what you want from the visit**: a diagnosis, better attack treatment, prevention, a referral, or reassurance.

The [doctor visit planner](tools/visit-prep.html) turns all of this into one printable page.

## Say the important thing first

Appointments are short. Start with the impact, in one or two sentences:

> "I've had headaches on about 10 days a month for a year. I've missed six work days in the last three months, and I'm taking ibuprofen most of those days."

That tells your doctor the frequency, the disability and the medication-overuse risk at once.

## Questions worth asking

- What type of headache do you think this is, and why?
- Do I need any tests?
- Could any of my medicines be making things worse?
- How often can I safely use my attack treatment?
- Would a preventive treatment make sense for me?
- What drug-free options could help?
- Which signs mean I should come back urgently?
- Should I see a neurologist or a headache clinic?

## Before you leave

Make sure you know the plan: what to take for an attack and how often, whether you are starting prevention and how long to give it, and when to come back. Most preventive treatments need six to twelve weeks at a proper dose to judge.

## When to ask for a specialist

Ask about a referral if the diagnosis is unclear, if you have tried two or three treatments without success, if you might have [cluster headache](types/cluster.html) or another rare type, or if headaches are badly affecting your life.
