Screens do not cause most headaches on their own, but long, still hours in front of them load the neck and shoulders, tire the eyes and keep stress high. Small changes to your setup and your day add up.

## Set up the screen

- **Height:** the top of the screen at or just below eye level, so you look slightly down without bending your neck.
- **Distance:** about an arm's length away. Increase text size rather than leaning in.
- **Laptops:** raise the laptop on a stand and use a separate keyboard and mouse. A laptop flat on a desk is the biggest single cause of forward-head posture.
- **Brightness:** match the screen to the room. A bright screen in a dark room, or a dim one in a bright office, makes the eyes work harder.
- **Glare:** place screens side-on to windows and avoid lights reflecting off them.

## Set up yourself

- Sit back in the chair with your lower back supported and feet flat.
- Keep elbows close to the body, at about a right angle.
- Notice your jaw: teeth should rest apart, tongue on the roof of the mouth.
- Get your eyes tested. An out-of-date prescription is a common, fixable cause of eye strain and headache.

## Set up your day

- **Every 20 minutes**, look about 20 feet (6 metres) away for 20 seconds.
- **Every 30 to 60 minutes**, stand, move and stretch your neck and shoulders for two minutes. The [desk-break timer](tools/desk-break.html) guides you.
- **Keep water on your desk** and eat lunch away from the screen.
- **Phones count.** Hold your phone higher rather than bending your neck down to it.

## What about blue light?

Blue-light filters and glasses are popular, but good evidence that they prevent headaches is lacking. Some people with light sensitivity find tinted lenses or dark mode more comfortable; if they help you, use them. This site's low-light mode is one click away at the top of every page.

## When it is more than posture

If neck pain and one-sided headache go together and neck movement sets it off, read about [cervicogenic headache](types/cervicogenic.html). If headaches come on most days despite these changes, see a doctor and check your [rebound risk](tools/rebound-risk.html).
