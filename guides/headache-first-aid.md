What you do in the first half hour of a headache often decides how the rest of the day goes. Work through these steps in order.

## 1. Check it is not an emergency

Stop and call your local emergency number if the headache came on suddenly and peaked within a minute, or if it comes with weakness, numbness, confusion, trouble speaking, loss of vision, fainting, a seizure, or fever with a stiff neck. After a head injury, vomiting or drowsiness also need urgent care. Not sure? Use the [warning-sign checker](tools/red-flags.html).

## 2. Treat early, if you treat

If you have a plan from your doctor, follow it, and take your usual treatment **early** rather than waiting to see how bad it gets. Most acute treatments, especially for [migraine](types/migraine.html), work better at the start. Note the time.

Keep an eye on how many days you treat. Treating on too many days can backfire: see [medication-overuse headache](types/medication-overuse.html).

## 3. Water and food

Drink a large glass of water. If you have missed a meal, eat something small with some protein. Dehydration and low blood sugar are two of the most common and most fixable triggers.

## 4. Change the room

- Lower the lights and close the blinds. On your phone, turn the brightness down or use this site's low-light mode.
- Reduce noise, or use earplugs.
- Get some fresh air if smells bother you.

## 5. Cold or heat

A cold pack wrapped in a cloth on the forehead or temples helps many people with migraine. Warmth on the neck and shoulders often helps [tension-type headache](types/tension-type.html). Try 15 minutes and see which suits you.

## 6. Loosen up

Unclench your jaw, drop your shoulders, and do a few gentle neck stretches. Two or three minutes of slow breathing, about six breaths a minute, calms the stress response. The [breathing pacer](tools/breathing.html) guides you.

## 7. Rest if you can

A short nap in a dark room ends some migraine attacks. If you need to keep working, take a five-minute break away from the screen every 20 to 30 minutes.

## 8. Log it

When you can, add an entry to your [headache diary](tools/headache-diary.html): when it started, what you took, what helped, and anything that might have set it off.

## Build a headache kit

Keep these together at home, at work and in your bag:

- your usual medicine, with a note of how many days you have used it this month;
- a water bottle and a small snack;
- an eye mask or sunglasses, and earplugs;
- a reusable cold pack or a cooling patch;
- a card with your doctor's details and any medicines you take.

## Call your doctor if

Your usual treatment stops working, headaches are becoming more frequent, or you need treatment on more than two or three days a week.
